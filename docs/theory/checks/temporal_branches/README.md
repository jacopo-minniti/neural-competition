# Sampled temporal peer-update interventions

This directory contains summaries and provenance for a new causal intervention
experiment, not a replot of observational interaction scores. See the
[report](../../../reports/temporal_branches.md).

The experiment covers all 245 original trajectories: 205 robustness runs in
13 settings, and 40 original 200k SGD/momentum runs. Replaying a baseline does
not add an independent initialization. Counterfactual branches are new
experiments from those same states.

## Reproduction

From the repository root:

```sh
.venv/bin/python scripts/studies/run_temporal_branches.py --match-width-dose
MPLCONFIGDIR=/tmp/neural-competition-matplotlib .venv/bin/python scripts/studies/analyze_temporal_branches.py
```

The fixed-20%-per-sender sensitivity comparison is separate:

```sh
.venv/bin/python scripts/studies/run_temporal_branches.py
MPLCONFIGDIR=/tmp/neural-competition-matplotlib .venv/bin/python scripts/studies/analyze_temporal_branches.py --design fixed_fraction
```

The [producer](../../../../scripts/studies/run_temporal_branches.py) defaults
to one CPU thread and resumes completed conditions. `--refresh` regenerates
a condition. `--conditions` selects a subset; `--limit-probes` is a development
smoke check and those incomplete conditions are excluded from summaries.
Original retained archives are read-only.

## What is measured

There are 25 prespecified, roughly logarithmic checkpoints and two uniformly
sampled senders per model/checkpoint. Each sender is damped for one actual
rounded update only; all freshly computed moments are retained. Subsequent
updates are ordinary SGD/momentum/Adam with the same future minibatches in
each branch. Each branch observes all recipients other than its sender.

The primary dose matches the removed prediction RMS across widths for each
training seed, checkpoint and sender rank, on the original fixed reference.
The target is the smallest feasible pulse at up to 20% sender damping. Zero
targets become no-ops at every width, rather than selecting active neurons.
A separate uniform-step control matches the removed reference prediction RMS.
Actual doses and numerical matching discrepancies are retained.

Every next-update outcome through 32 steps is measured; five of the 25
checkpoints extend to 128 steps. Prefix sums are exact over these windows.
Outcomes are retained at lags 1/4/16/32 and, for that subset, 128. The lag
figure uses the same five checkpoints at every horizon. Individual recipient
effects are retained at lags 1/32/128.

The fixed reference is the original 256- or 512-example training subset.
Held-out outcomes use 256 test examples selected with RNG seed 20261018,
never used in training or in dose calibration. All losses are half-MSE.

## Versioned outputs

- `episode_contrasts.csv.gz`: paired causal outcomes per seed, checkpoint,
  sender, lag and evaluation view.
- `summary_per_seed.csv.gz`: pooled numerators/ordinary recipient helpful gains
  within each seed, and absolute effect magnitudes.
- `summary.csv.gz`: seed means and pointwise 95% bootstrap intervals.
- `width_contrasts.csv.gz`: paired wide-minus-narrow contrasts, with seed intervals.
- `coverage.csv.gz`: checkpoint/episode counts for every reported scope.
- `recipient_effects_per_seed.csv.gz` and `recipient_effects.csv.gz`: positive and
  negative individual-recipient effects, before cancellation, at selected lags.
- `provenance/`: condition-specific input hashes, original model lists,
  checkpoint/RNG audits, exact branch checks, settings and measured runtimes.
- `manifest.json`: complete analysis inputs/hashes and overall scope.
- `fixed_fraction/`: corresponding sensitivity summaries for fixed 20% pulses.

The larger condition-level tables, neuron records and dose calibrations are
kept locally under `runs/temporal_branches/matched_dose/` and
`runs/temporal_branches/fixed_fraction/`. They are ignored, like the original
retained arrays, and regenerated by the producer. Their input hashes are in
the versioned manifests. Executed producer copies are kept under
`runs/temporal_branches/executed_sources/`; versioned copies accompany these
summaries for source-hash audits.

## Signs, uncertainty and limits

Positive `peer_harm_benefit` means sender-damped cumulative recipient H minus
ordinary H is positive: allowing the ordinary sender update prevented later
harm. Negative means damping the sender reduced harm. This does not assume
that a positive same-step pair cost is destructive competition.

`per100G` divides by ordinary cumulative recipient helpful gain in the same
future windows. `per100Pulse` divides by the sum of absolute ordinary-versus-
damped loss differences immediately after the intervention. The latter
measures the response relative to the pulse's initial objective impact, not
a percentage of training improvement. Both pool within seed before averaging
seeds; individual near-zero ratios are not averaged.

All 25 checkpoints receive equal sampling weight, on an approximately log-time
schedule. These are sampled-window summaries, not cumulative effects over the
entire run. The shared-loss sensitivity uses reference MSE .0766–.2; the
`first_shared_loss` scope uses the earliest sampled checkpoint in that band.
This is coarse phase conditioning, not exact loss matching. Check coverage
before using any subset as a full-cohort comparison.

`late_sampled` restricts to checkpoints with at least 1,000 completed updates.
`well_matched` drops a whole seed/checkpoint/sender-rank comparison if any width
has more than 5% cross-width or uniform pulse-calibration error. Individual
recipient classes use zero-threshold antecedent A/J signs; their tables are
not reproductions of the earlier tolerance-based strict-event frequencies.

The `completed` field is the count before the modified update. Lag 1 is the
first subsequent ordinary update, two updates after that recorded count.

Intervals use 4,000 seed-bootstrap draws, RNG 741. There are five original
seeds per robustness setting and ten per 200k setting. Recipients, checkpoints
and branches within a seed are correlated and are not independent replicates.
Intervals are exploratory and not adjusted for the many comparisons.

The residual/proposal split is an exact symmetric accounting split of the
observed prediction changes. At lag 1, untouched recipient parameters coincide
across ordinary/local branches, allowing a true common-model comparison of
their candidate updates. At later lags parameters differ; the split then
compares prediction changes against common residuals and is not a parameter
transplant or an independently identified causal mediation effect.
